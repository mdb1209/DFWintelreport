United Musculoskeletal Partners (UMP) Network
Understanding the platform behind all five accounts
What is UMP?
United Musculoskeletal Partners is a physician-owned management services organization (MSO) - the nation's leading MSK MSO. Formed in December 2021 by Resurgens Orthopaedics. UMP handles billing, management, and business functions under a shared platform while practices retain clinical independence.
✅ The Baylor Partnership - Updated
Baylor Scott & White has officially signed a joint venture contract with UMP, making these practices official Baylor partners. No rebranding will occur - the practices retain their existing names and identities. Additionally, most providers across the network are already Tier 1 providers under the Baylor Quality Alliance (BQA), BSW's physician performance network that recognizes high-quality, cost-efficient care. This JV reinforces the practices' strategic alignment with BSW's value-based care priorities.

Note: These practices remain independent group practices under UMP's MSO umbrella - they are distinct from BSW's directly employed physician network (BSW Orthopedic Associates of Dallas, BSW Orthopaedic & Spine Hospital Arlington). The BSW/Luna "Therapy at Home" competitive threat still applies and should be addressed separately.

SWHR ranked #3 nationally in Medicare ACO savings. BQA participation varies by practice - see individual profiles for tier detail. Competitive Threat: BSW + Luna "Therapy at Home"
What you're up against and why it matters
🏢 BSW / Luna - "Therapy at Home"
  • Baylor Scott & White Institute for Rehabilitation + Luna (on-demand PT company)
  • Therapist travels to patient's home with equipment
  • Bills Medicare Part B outpatient (POS 12 - home)
  • Patient pays 20% coinsurance + $283 deductible
  • Therapy ONLY - no nursing, no clinician-supported ADLs
  • No homebound requirement - available to more patients
  • Cheaper per episode for CMS (~$1,300–$2,100 vs. your $2,000–$4,400)
  • Lower overhead for BSW/Luna = profitable business model
✅ Your Home Health Model
  • Bills Part A or Part B via home health benefit
  • Patient pays $0 - Medicare covers 100%
  • Nursing + Therapy in one coordinated plan of care
  • Skilled nursing catches DVT, wound complications, medication issues
  • Bundled PDGM payment covers all disciplines
  • Strong argument in bundled payment models (CJR-X coming 2027)
  • Cannot run simultaneously with outpatient Part B therapy
  • Requires homebound documentation

Therapy at Home Competition - BSW / Luna
Understanding the competitive model, billing mechanics, and your counter-argument
⚠️ Who Is Doing This
The specific "Therapy at Home" program is Baylor Scott & White Institute for Rehabilitation's "Outpatient at Home" service, operated through a partnership with Luna - a venture-backed on-demand physical therapy company. Luna matches patients with therapists who travel to the home. BSW/Luna markets this throughout most of North Texas via the BSW physician network.
How They Bill - The Mechanics
🔶 BSW / Luna Model - Outpatient Part B
  • Bills Medicare Part B outpatient using standard CPT codes
  • Place of Service code: 12 (Home)
  • Claims filed on CMS-1500 form - same as clinic billing
  • Reimbursed at Medicare Physician Fee Schedule (MPFS) rates - same as a brick-and-mortar clinic
  • No facility overhead requirement to bill POS 12 - CMS doesn't distinguish
  • Lower overhead for BSW/Luna (no building, equipment) = high margins
  • CANNOT bill alongside home health (Part A) for the same patient at the same time
  • No homebound requirement - available to a broader patient population
  • Therapy only - PT, OT, SLP. No nursing. No clinician-supported ADL assistance.
✅ Your Model - Home Health Benefit
  • Bills through Medicare home health benefit - Part A or Part B
  • Reimbursed via PDGM (Patient-Driven Groupings Model) - bundled 30-day periods
  • One payment covers all disciplines: nursing + PT + OT - clinicians address ADLs directly
  • Patient cost: $0 - Medicare covers 100%
  • Skilled nursing included in plan of care - catches post-op complications
  • Homebound status required - typically met immediately post-surgery
  • Cannot run simultaneously with outpatient Part B therapy billing
  • Strong compliance and documentation requirements (OASIS, plan of care, physician certification)

A typical post-total joint patient receiving 16 visits of BSW/Luna outpatient therapy will owe $550–$750 out of pocket on top of their surgical co-insurance. Your home health patients pay $0. For a patient on a fixed income, this is a real decision factor. Ask the surgeon: "Would you rather your patients receive equivalent or better care with no out-of-pocket expense, or have them pay $600+ and not tell you they're skipping visits because of cost?"
2
We provide nursing. They don't. BSW/Luna sends a therapist. You send a nurse and a therapist. For post-surgical patients, nursing catches the complications that send them back to the OR or the ER - DVT signs, wound dehiscence, fever, abnormal drainage, medication issues. One caught complication saves the surgeon a readmission, a potential liability issue, and a patient outcome that shows up in their quality data.
3
Their therapy and our home health cannot overlap - the choice happens at discharge. This is a crucial nuance. A patient can't be on both a home health plan of care (Part A) AND receiving outpatient therapy at home (Part B) simultaneously. The referral decision made at the hospital or surgery center is the decision. If BSW/Luna gets there first, you don't. Position with the discharge planner and surgical coordinator, not just the physician.
4
The "why" behind billing outpatient from the home. If a surgeon asks why BSW/Luna can bill outpatient rates without a clinic - it's a fair and somewhat unresolved policy question. CMS allows POS 12 billing (home) at the same MPFS rate as a clinic. There's no overhead requirement in the fee schedule. What this means: BSW/Luna has dramatically lower costs than a clinic but collects the same reimbursement. That's a profitable model for them, not a benefit to the patient or the payer.
5
We are appropriate for the post-surgical window. They are not. Outpatient therapy at home is technically intended for patients who are not homebound but want convenience. A fresh post-TKA or THA patient typically IS homebound. Home health is the clinically appropriate care pathway for the first 30-60 days. BSW/Luna's model makes more sense as a post-home health step-down, not a replacement for it.

The Bundled Payment Connection - Why Surgeons Have Skin in the Game
CJR-X - Coming October 1, 2027
CMS proposed Comprehensive Care for Joint Replacement Expanded (CJR-X) - the first mandatory, nationwide bundled payment model in Medicare history. Comment period closed June 9, 2026. Final rule expected August 2026. More than 2,500 hospitals will be required to participate.

Under CJR-X, hospitals own the entire 90-day Medicare episode - every Part A and Part B dollar from surgery through 90 days post-discharge. That includes SNF, home health, outpatient therapy, physician visits, readmissions, everything.
Post-Acute Care Cost Hierarchy
SNF Stay: $8,000–$20,000+ (30 days)
Home Health (Your Model): $2,000–$4,400
Outpatient Therapy at Home: $1,300–$2,100
At 300 joint replacements/year, choosing outpatient therapy over home health = $120,000–$690,000 in episode savings - some of which flows back to the surgeon via gainsharing.
How Surgeon Gainsharing Works Under CJR-X
  • Hospitals set a target price per episode with CMS
  • If the hospital's actual episode spending comes in below target → reconciliation payment to hospital
  • Hospitals can enter gainsharing arrangements with collaborating physicians (surgeons)
  • Surgeons receive a share of savings when their patients' episodes come in under target
  • A surgeon routing 300 patients to cheaper post-acute care (outpatient therapy vs. home health) saves ~$120K–$690K in episode costs - directly increasing their gainsharing check
  • Gainsharing is subject to caps and quality requirements - cannot be tied to referral volume
  • This is a CMS-sanctioned, policy-approved financial incentive
How You Win This Conversation
Your counter-argument - using CMS's own data against the cost-savings narrative
1
One readmission cancels years of post-acute savings. Under CJR-X, a single readmission within 90 days falls on the hospital's episode tab - $15,000–$25,000 per event. The post-acute savings from routing 5 patients to outpatient therapy instead of home health (~$2,500–$10,000) evaporates instantly with one DVT that became a PE, one wound infection that wasn't caught, one patient who fell because no nurse was monitoring their gait. Skilled nursing in the home is the most cost-effective readmission prevention tool available.
2
CMS's own CJR data says home health is where smart hospitals went. The NEJM two-year evaluation of the original CJR found that hospitals reduced costs primarily by shifting patients away from SNFs - and home health utilization actually increased under bundled payments. Hospitals determined that home health with nursing was a better readmission prevention investment than continuing down the care intensity ladder to therapy-only. The research supports your model, not BSW/Luna's.
3
Bring your readmission rate data. This is the most powerful tool in this conversation. If you can show a surgeon: "Our home health patients for TKA/THA have a X% 30-day readmission rate vs. the national average of X%" - that's a real number they can plug into their CJR-X episode savings model. Quality and cost are not correlated - Trillian Health's analysis of CJR-X found that lower episode cost does not mean better outcomes. Your differentiation is: same or better outcomes, lower readmission risk, competitive episode cost.
4
Frame your service as a CJR-X partnership, not a commodity. The UMP practices and the hospitals they operate out of will all be subject to CJR-X by October 2027. Position yourself now as a home health partner who understands bundled payments, communicates outcomes back to the surgical team, and can demonstrate episode-level performance. BSW/Luna can't do that - they're a therapy-only service with no nursing layer and no episode accountability.
5
Know the patient cost argument cold. Even surgeons who don't care about their own savings will care when their patients come back at the 2-week follow-up complaining about a $600 therapy bill. "Your patients pay $0 with us. BSW/Luna charges them 20% coinsurance after their deductible. For a patient on Medicare with no supplemental plan, that's a real hardship." Patient satisfaction and billing complaints affect surgeon reputation and referral patterns back to the practice.
